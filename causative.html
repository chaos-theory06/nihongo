Causative 使役形しえきけい

三つみっつの違ちがう意味いみがあります: (There are three different meanings:)

  1. Enforcement (強制きょうせい) To make or force someone to do something.
  2. Permission (許可きょか) To let or allow someone to do something.
  3. Induction / Emotion (誘発ゆうはつ) To cause someone to feel or experience something.

もうちょっと詳くわしく見みてみます: (Let's look in a little more detail:)

Enforcement (強制きょうせい)

お母かあさんは娘むすめに皿さらを洗あらわせます。
The mother makes her daughter wash the dishes.

The daughter doesn't want to do it, so this is an example of enforcement.

Another example:

The mother tells her child: “You must not play games.” In other words, she says that playing games is not allowed. This is the negative form.

What is the positive form of しません?

  • します → させます
  • しません → させません

So, how do we say this in a sentence? What does the mother say to her son?

お母かあさんは息子むすこにゲームをさせません。

Permission (許可きょか)

Example 1: Making sweets

In an ordinary sentence:

子供こどもはお菓子かしを作つくります。
The child makes sweets.

When we change this into a causative sentence expressing permission, it becomes:

お母かあさんは子供こどもにお菓子かしを作つくらせます。
The mother lets the child make sweets.

Notice the changes:

  • 作つくります → 作つくらせます
  • 子供こどもは → 子供こどもに

The verb 作つくります changes to 作つくらせます, and because the child becomes the person who is allowed to do the action, the particle は changes to に.

Example 2: Having a phone

The second sentence is:

子供こどもは電話でんわを持もちます。
The child has a phone.

When we change it into the causative form:

  • 持もちます → 持もたせます
  • 子供こどもは → 子供こどもに

The complete sentence becomes:

お父とうさんは子供こどもに電話でんわを持もたせます。
The father lets his child have a phone.

Important explanation

In this situation, just as in the first example with the mother, the father is in a higher position than the child. The person in the higher position allows the person in the lower position to do something they want to do. In other words, the person in the higher position gives permission and lets the other person do something.

やらせてあげます。
This means: “I let them do it” or “I allow them to do it.”

Induction / Emotion (誘発ゆうはつ)

Example: “She is angry. She won't go on a date with me tomorrow. What should I do?” — 彼女かのじょは怒おこっている。明日あしたデートしてくれない。どうしよう

So, we say:

彼かれは彼女かのじょを怒おこらせました。
He made her angry.

Now, let's look at some other verbs that describe feelings or emotions.

Emotion-related verbs

Original verb Causative form English
安心あんしんする安心あんしんさせるTo reassure / put someone at ease
困こまる困こまらせるTo trouble / cause someone difficulty
心配しんぱいする心配しんぱいさせるTo make someone worry
楽たのしむ楽たのしませるTo entertain / make someone enjoy themselves
満足まんぞくする満足まんぞくさせるTo satisfy someone
笑わらう笑わらわせるTo make someone laugh

And there are many other verbs like these. With emotion-related verbs, the causative form often means to cause someone to feel a particular emotion.

For example:

  • 彼かれを安心あんしんさせます。 — I reassure him.
  • 彼女かのじょを心配しんぱいさせました。 — I made her worry.
  • 子供こどもを笑わらわせました。 — I made the child laugh.
Remember the difference:
  • 怒おこる — To get angry
  • 怒おこらせる — To make someone angry

This is the third meaning of the Japanese causative form (使役形しえきけい): causing an emotion or reaction in another person.
