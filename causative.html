Causative 使役形しえきけい

三つみっつの違ちがう意味いみがあります: (There are three different meanings:)

  1. Enforcement (強制きょうせい) To make or force someone to do something.
  2. Permission (許可きょか) To let or allow someone to do something.
  3. Induction / Emotion (誘発ゆうはつ) To cause someone to feel or experience something.

もうちょっと詳くわしく見みてみます: (Let's look in a little more detail:)

Induction / Emotion (誘発ゆうはつ)

Example: “She is angry. She won't go on a date with me tomorrow. What should I do?” — 彼女かのじょは怒おこっている。明日あしたデートしてくれない。どうしよう

So, we say:

彼かれは彼女かのじょを怒おこらせました。
He made her angry.

Now, let's look at some other verbs that describe feelings or emotions.

Emotion-related verbs

Original verb Causative form English
安心あんしんする安心あんしんさせるTo reassure / put someone at ease
困こまる困こまらせるTo trouble / cause someone difficulty
心配しんぱいする心配しんぱいさせるTo make someone worry
楽たのしむ楽たのしませるTo entertain / make someone enjoy themselves
満足まんぞくする満足まんぞくさせるTo satisfy someone
笑わらう笑わらわせるTo make someone laugh

And there are many other verbs like these. With emotion-related verbs, the causative form often means to cause someone to feel a particular emotion.

For example:

  • 彼かれを安心あんしんさせます。 — I reassure him.
  • 彼女かのじょを心配しんぱいさせました。 — I made her worry.
  • 子供こどもを笑わらわせました。 — I made the child laugh.
Remember the difference:
  • 怒おこる — To get angry
  • 怒おこらせる — To make someone angry

This is the third meaning of the Japanese causative form (使役形しえきけい): causing an emotion or reaction in another person.
